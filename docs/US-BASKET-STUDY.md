# US structural price-inefficiency study

The user requested a stronger focus on finding and exploiting inefficiencies. This study directly tests complete US weather outcome ladders, independently of the forecast model. A complete exclusive and exhaustive temperature partition pays one dollar in total for equal quantities of every long outcome, or N-1 dollars for equal quantities of every short outcome. That conditional payout identity does not make sequential execution risk-free.

Registration: `data/us-baskets/1789691752181318000/registration.json`. It references the nine September 8–16 NYC events from `data/us-benter/1789690917159502000`. At registration, none of those test histories had yet downloaded. This is a new structural hypothesis registered before analyzing the test prices; it does not change the Benter study's arms or reuse the prior six-date pilot as a new holdout.

Signal times are a one-minute grid from 20 minutes before to 20 minutes after the previously defined forecast decision time (six hours before the fixed-standard-time climate day). All six histories must have observations at most 60 seconds old, with no more than 10 seconds between leg timestamps. The first positive after-fee basket margin of at least $0.10 becomes an intention. Every leg must then have a new observation at least 60 seconds after that intention. The earliest complete coherent refreshed ladder within five additional minutes is evaluated; the basket is canceled if its margin disappears. Later prices are not used to choose the best earlier fill. Each direction/scenario permits at most one completed basket per date.

Each independent account starts at $50 with a $40 reserve and a $5 total basket budget. All legs use the same whole-contract quantity, and all leg fees count against that budget. The current US taker fee is counterfactually applied to historical prices; zero and half-cent per-side slippage are separate scenarios. No exchange portfolio-margin optimization, token splitting, merging, bridge, CTF, or UMA mechanics are used. All legs are fully funded in the simulator. Cash release is assumed next-day 11 AM ET, with exact historical settlement timing unverified.

For every candidate, the evaluator enumerates all nonempty proper subsets of filled legs and every possible winning band. `partial_fill_worst_pnl` is the minimum resulting terminal P&L; this exposes legging risk rather than applying the complete-basket floor to incomplete fills. Historical quote depth and atomic completion remain unverified. Returns are display-price scenarios, not executable-arbitrage or live-profit evidence.

Run `tools/run_us_baskets.py STUDY_DIRECTORY` only after the referenced collection completes, then `tools/audit_us_baskets.py ANALYSIS_DIRECTORY`. The output preserves coverage, exclusions, intentions that failed delayed checks, completed scenarios, per-leg ledgers, source/code hashes and a profit graph. No tuning on those nine dates; report zero signals and negative results as faithfully as positive ones.

Completed output: `data/us-baskets/1789691752181318000/analysis-1789692289657330000`. Across 3,499 price observations and 54 verified payouts, all nine ladders had unit aggregate settlement. There were 369 scheduled decision times per scenario: 265 lacked a complete, fresh, sufficiently synchronized ladder; the remaining 104 had no $0.10 after-cost payout margin. Both basket directions, with and without half-cent slippage, therefore had zero qualifying signals, zero positions and $0 trading P&L. The allocated subscription was $64.54 per independent scenario. The audit passed source, code and summary checks; fill/partial-fill accounting branches were exercised with synthetic unit-test examples, not claimed historical trades.

This does not prove US baskets never misprice. It rejects an opportunity claim for the actual covered observations in this short sample. Future investigation needs prospectively captured simultaneous depth and broader registered event coverage, rather than relaxing timing until a profitable historical artifact appears.
