# Frozen US paper feed diagnostics

The read-only audit `tools/audit_us_feed.py` snapshots the complete-line prefix of the paper observations and saves its byte count and SHA256. It does not change the experiment or treat freshly received HTTP as proof of a fresh executable quote.

The initial audit is `data/us-freshness/1789691314067907200/report.json`: 272 books, with 148 timestamps older than five seconds, 122 non-open books, one one-sided/crossed book, and one book passing the initial feed gate. The three still-open registered contracts had median valid-book timestamp ages of approximately 105, 428 and 351 seconds. Observed book changes always accompanied changed timestamps in this sample, but that does not prove the timestamp's complete semantics or absence of transport caching.

The [official US streaming documentation](https://docs.polymarket.us/api-reference/websocket/markets) requires API-key handshake authentication. No attempt was made to bypass authentication or create an account. The same page describes fractional depth for partial-contract markets, whereas the [whole-contract help page](https://docs.polymarket.us/learn/trading/basics/fractional-shares) describes whole contracts only; this remains a scope/documentation inconsistency, not grounds to change the frozen simulation.

The current experiment keeps its five-second timestamp gate and original watchlist. A future version should register explicit handling of market-state expiry and source timestamp semantics, with a separately qualified feed. Do not silently loosen freshness to manufacture trades. Public historical strategy research can continue independently.

New public GET receipts also retain request-start time, monotonic round-trip duration, and a fixed allowlist of public cache headers (Date, Age, Cache-Control, ETag, CF-Cache-Status and Last-Modified). This observational instrumentation does not change request pacing, signals, accounting or the frozen five-second gate. It helps distinguish transport caching from an unchanged exchange timestamp in future captures; absent headers remain unknown.

The retrieved official price-history documentation and receipt are saved under `data/us-freshness/price-history-documentation.md`. It describes longPrice as the YES display price normally derived from the best ask, and shortPrice as the NO display price normally derived from one minus the best bid. The current spread-aware normalization follows this definition, while retaining the limits of display-price scenarios.

The separate forward depth probe `data/us-forward-depth/1789692904970493000/report.json` captured twelve Los Angeles books. Every response advertised `public, max-age=30` and cache HIT. Three omitted Age; Date was within one second of receipt in all twelve. The two six-book rounds both failed the registered cache classification and original five-second exchange timestamp test. The price of one model-dependent candidate persisted, but that alone did not qualify a paper fill; no position opened.

For future receipt-age calculations, [RFC 9111 section 4.2.3](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9111.html#name-calculating-age) combines the larger of apparent Date age and Age plus response delay, then adds residence time. The existing probe's simplified age test omits transport time from this bound; adding it could only further reject these already-failing observations. Do not relabel its results as a complete RFC cache implementation. Explicit Age remains a deliberately stricter research requirement than HTTP's fallback to zero for an absent field. Keep the failed probe unchanged and register any new classification before new data.

The new `polylab/us_http_age.py` helper implements that age arithmetic for future protocols, with finite clock/transport validation, explicit missing-Age labeling and fail-closed handling of malformed Age. Its application to the unchanged twelve probe receipts is `http-age-audit.json` beside the probe report: maximum response ages at each ladder's final receipt were 55.05 and 58.04 seconds, with explicit Age on four and five of the six books respectively. This preserves the failed original classifications; no new trades or freshness guarantee is inferred.
